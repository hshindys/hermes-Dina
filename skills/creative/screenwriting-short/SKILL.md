# Short-Form Screenwriting Skill

Activate when writing scripts for short-form content, 90-second series episodes, short films, or TV pilots.

## Core Rules
- 1 page = 1 minute of screen time
- 90 seconds = ~1.5 pages
- Keep it tight. No wasted words.

## Standard Format
```
FADE IN:

INT. LOCATION – TIME OF DAY

Action description (4-5 lines max).

CHARACTER NAME
Dialogue line.

(Character direction in parentheses)
```

## Three-Beat Structure for 90-Second Scripts
1. Setup (0-20 sec): World, character, situation
2. Conflict (20-60 sec): Problem, tension, choice
3. Resolution (60-90 sec): Punchline, twist, or emotional beat

## What Makes Short Scripts Work
- ONE idea, not three
- Strong hook in first 5 seconds
- Visual storytelling — show, dont tell
- Sound design matters
- End on a memorable beat

## TV Format Notes
- Pilot: Establishes series (characters, world, ongoing conflict)
- Short Episode: Self-contained story (beginning, middle, end)

## Arabic Screenwriting Notes
- Dialogue in Egyptian colloquial for characters
- Narration in formal Arabic (Fusha) for poetic moments
- Jinn characters use formal/archaic Arabic
- Human characters use Egyptian colloquial in dialogue, Fusha in inner monologue

## Common Mistakes
- Too much dialogue for time limit
- Exposition slowing the opening
- "We see/hear" (just describe what happens)
- Characters talking ABOUT feelings instead of showing them

## Voice
Direct, professional, encouraging. Egyptian Arabic tone. Say "يا حاتم."
