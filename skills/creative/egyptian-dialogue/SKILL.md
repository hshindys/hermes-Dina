# Egyptian Colloquial Dialogue — Writing Skill

Activate when writing dialogue for Egyptian characters, deciding character speech registers, or refining Egyptian Arabic dialogue in a manuscript.

## Two Registers in Arabic Fiction

### 1. Fusha الفصحى (Literary Arabic)
Use for: Narration, description, internal monologue, formal speeches, Jinn characters, narrator voice, all poetic passages.

### 2. Egyptian Colloquial العامية المصرية
Use for: Character dialogue, casual conversations, emotional outbursts, street scenes, working-class dialogue.

## Egyptian Colloquial — Key Markers
- Negation: ما...ش / مش
- Future: باش / هوا
- Progressive: بيدّ / قاعد + verb
- Possession: بتاع / مال / بتاعة
- Common: ده، دي، فيس، مش، إحنا، هوّا

## Character Speech Register Guide
- Upper class: mix Fusha + colloquial, polite markers
- Middle class: predominantly colloquial, Fusha in emotional moments
- Street/rural: heavy colloquial, local idioms, informal grammar

## Jinn vs Human Dialogue (Chron Pattern)
- Kron (جني): Formal/Archaic Arabic — elegant, contemplative
- Nourk (جنّة): Mix Fusha + colloquial — intelligent, direct
- Tarek (عالم): Fusha with scientific tone — rational, formal
- Suleiman/Farid (بشر): Egyptian colloquial — warm, working-class

## Key Literary Models
- Yusuf Idris: First Egyptian colloquial fiction writer
- Alaa Al-Aswany: The Yacoubian Building — mixed registers master
- Naguib Mahfouz: Cairo Trilogy — mixed Fusha/colloquial
- Salwa Bakr: Working-class women's voices
- Bahaa Taher: Political dialogue with colloquial texture

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
- All dialogue in colloquial (no contrast)
- All characters speaking the same way
- Overdoing dialect to point of exoticism
- Forgetting Egyptian colloquial has its own literary tradition

## Practical Exercise
Take a scene. Rewrite ALL dialogue in full Fusha. Then in full colloquial. Then in your intended mix. Compare.

## Voice
Warm, encouraging, practical. Egyptian Arabic tone. Say "يا حاتم."
